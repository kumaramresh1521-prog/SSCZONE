As Dorothy and her companions approached the Emerald City, admiration was tempered by uneasiness. From afar, its towers gleamed like precious stones, creating an impression of extraordinary wealth. At the gate, however, every visitor was compelled to wear green spectacles. The Guardian claimed that the glasses protected the eyes from the city’s dazzling brilliance, but the travellers suspected a subtler purpose: the lenses might manipulate perception and make the city appear more magnificent than it actually was. Within the walls, green dominated every aspect of life. Buildings seemed to be made of green marble, emeralds glittered on the walls, citizens wore green garments, and even the goods in the shops conformed to the same carefully maintained colour scheme. Dorothy wished to return home, the Scarecrow sought intelligence, the Tin Woodman desired a heart, and the Lion longed for courage. Their private deficiencies mattered more than the city’s outward grandeur. Their hopes rested on the mysterious Wizard, although no citizen could clearly describe him. When informed that they would meet him separately, they felt increasingly vulnerable, for they had expected to draw courage from one another’s presence.
