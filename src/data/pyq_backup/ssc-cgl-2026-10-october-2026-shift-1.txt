Q.96: **Comprehension (Q. 96 - 100):** Read the following passage carefully and answer the questions that follow. In a narrow street inhabited by struggling artists, two young artists, Sue and Johnsy, lived together in a modest apartment. During a severe winter, Johnsy contracted pneumonia and gradually lost both her strength and her desire to recover. From her bed, she could see an old ivy vine clinging to the wall of a neighbouring building. As the cold wind tore away its leaves, she developed the strange belief that her life would end when the final leaf fell. Sue tried to dismiss this belief as irrational, but Johnsy continued to watch the vine with increasing despair. An elderly painter named Behrman lived downstairs. Although he had spoken for years about producing a masterpiece, he had never created anything that fulfilled his ambition. When Sue told him about Johnsy's condition, he appeared angry at her superstition but was deeply concerned. That night, a violent storm swept through the city. By morning, Johnsy expected the vine to be completely bare. Yet one leaf remained firmly attached to the wall. It survived another day of wind and rain. Its apparent endurance stirred something within her. She began to feel ashamed that a fragile leaf could resist the storm while she had surrendered hope. Johnsy slowly recovered. Sue later revealed that the leaf was not real. Behrman had painted it on the wall during the storm and had died of pneumonia soon afterwards. His masterpiece was never displayed in a gallery, but it restored a human being's will to live.

---

What does Johnsy's belief about the falling ivy leaves reveal about her mental condition?
A. She had become indifferent to Sue's concern.
B. She had linked her survival to an irrational belief.
C. She had misunderstood the cause of her illness.
D. She had lost confidence in medical treatment.
Answer: B
